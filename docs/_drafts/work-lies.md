We lie to children. One of these lies is,

> Work hard to get ahead.

The sentiment here is that if you work hard then you will reap the
rewards. If you diligently work, first at your study, then at your
job, then you'll be well rewarded. It is something I have followed my
entire life, but in my experience it just isn't how the world
works. I'd like to illustrate what I mean with a hypothetical story, a
parable, about our protagonist, Bob.

Bob has worked hard all his life - in school, university and then in
his job. In each of these areas he is a high achiever. He sacrifices
his personal time to do the best he can - to always have his
assignments in, to do the best of every presentation and every
project. And he's good at it. In each of them he excels. He
sacrificaes, and his work always goes above and beyond.

To a certain extent the saying is true. Bob will always have a
job. There's always going to be someone willing to employ someone who
works his butt off, and so Bob is always able to earn money. To that
extent the adage is true. But Bob also attracts Eve.

Eve is the opposite of Bob. Her skill in life is to claim credit (or
deny blame) for others work. And when Eve meets Bob she has struck an
absolute goldmine - a constant source of excellent work which she can
exploit. Eve uses her skills to present Bob's hard work as if it was
her own.

A constant topic of Eve's conversation is all the amazing work that
she has apparently done, without any mention of Bob. When Bob talks to
others he is puzzled that they seem to know about the work he's doing,
but surprised they don't know he has anything at all to do with
it. But Bob is busy and so it just seems odd. But it's a successful
strategy for Eve, who is promoted to a position above Bob, for
consistently excellent work.

As a superior, Eve gets to report what work everyone under her is
doing, including Bob. Eve openly talks about all the work as if it is
her work alone. Bob rationalises this to himself as when she says that
"she" did it, everyone understands it's her team. But in official
reports things are worse. All the reporting goes through Eve, so Bob
doesn't even see when she puts her own name down on Bob's
projects. Bob is not mentioned as having anything to do with many of
them at all - or if he is mentioned, he's put down as responsible for
a very small fraction, at most 20% or 30% of his actual contribution.

It looks, on paper, as if Bob's workload is a tiny fraction of what
he's actually doing. So every time there's a new project or task, it
is assigned to Bob. Bob's responsibliities rapibly expands, with
nobody outside the team wise that he's fast becoming overworked. Over
many years, Bob's workload goes well beyond his official hours: 70,
80, 90 hours. The workload keeps increasing. But Bob thinks that if he
works hard, he'll get ahead, so he keeps going, dutifully believing
his work is appreciated. When it starts to regularly hit 100 hours a
week, week after week, year after year, even Bob starts to get burnt
out.

But what can Bob do? It's hard for him to cut down, because he's not
officially doing any of the work he is actually doing. When he goes to
HR, they flatly insist that, no, he's not actually even involved in
the project which he's been working on for the last five years. The
only person who fully knows it's actually him is Eve.

By this time, Bob has been working in a position way above his pay
grade for many years now. If he quit, he'd be back a decade of work,
leaving behind the castles that he's nutured and built, applying for
positions well below the level he's actually been working at (even if
that's unackowledged). In fact, he can't even talk about the work he's
been doing for a significant fraction of his life, or it will be
contradicted by the official record and Eve. And by this point Eve is
telling him point blank not to talk about all the work he's
done. After all it's far in excess of what anyone 'should' have done -
and he doesn't want to be in trouble for violating company policy. So
Bob continues.

In the end it's not so much the level of physical work that gets to
Bob - although Bob is older now, and such long working weeks (and
weekends) really aren't physically sustainable. What gets to Bob is
the psycologocial pressure. Doing work today that tomorrow Alice will
claim she did, and he knows it. He knows he won't be able to say
tomorrow what he did today. He's living a shadow of a real life -
where he almost doesn't exist. Bob regrets all the sacrifice that he's
made- all that hard work over many years- for others gain.

We tell our children lies. Work hard to get ahead is one of them. If
the question is being lazy or not, by all means hard work is
better. But in any sizeable organisation, whether it's government or
private, if it's big enough always a large number of 'Eves'. People
who are better at climbing the greasy pole than actually providing
anything of value. People who are not constrained to act in moral
ways, such as accurately attributing work, if that gets in the way of
their own advancement.
