We lie to children. One of these lies is,

> Work hard to get ahead.

The sentiment here is that if you work hard then you will reap the
rewards. However, this just isn't how the world works. I'd like to
illustrate what I mean with a hypothetical story, a parable, about our
protagonist, Bob.

</p>


Bob has worked hard all his life - in school, university and then in
his job. In each of these areas he is a high achiever. He sacrificed
his personal time to do the best possible - to always have his
assignments in, to do the best of every presentation and every
project. And is good at it. In each of them he excels. He
sacrifices mean that his work always goes above and beyond.

Bob will always have a job. There's always going to be someone willing
to employ someone who works his butt off, and so Bob is always able to
earn money. To that extent the adage is true. He has got ahead. But
Bob also attracts Eve.

Eve is the opposite of Bob. Her skill in life is to claim credit (or
deny blame) for others' work. And when Eve meets Bob she has struck an
absolute goldmine - a constant source of excellent work which she can
exploit. Eve uses her skills to present Bob's hard work as if it was
her own.

A constant topic of Eve's conversation is all the amazing work that
she has apparently done, but without any mention of Bob. When Bob
talks to others he is puzzled that they seem to know about the work
he's doing, but surprised they don't know he has anything at all to do
with it. But Bob is busy and so it just seems odd. But it's a
successful strategy for Eve, who is promoted to a position above Bob,
for her consistently excellent work.

As a superior, Eve gets to report what work everyone under her is
doing, including Bob. Eve openly talks about all the work as if it is
her work alone. Bob rationalises this to himself as when she says that
"she" did it, everyone understands it's her team. But in official
reports things are worse. All the reporting goes through Eve, so Bob
doesn't even see when she puts her own name down on Bob's
projects. Bob is not mentioned as having anything to do with many of
them at all - or if he is mentioned, he's put down as responsible for
a very small fraction, at most 20% or 30% of his actual contribution.

It looks to those higher up, as if Bob's workload is a tiny fraction
of what he's actually doing. That's bad for two reasons. First, he's
not being acknowledged for the things he is achieving. Secondly, to
every superior except Eve, it looks like Bob is not actually busy. So,
every time there's a new project or task, it is assigned to Bob. Bob's
responsibilities continually expand: 80, 90, even 100 hours per week,
with nobody outside the team aware. But Bob thinks that if he works
hard, he'll get ahead, so he keeps going, dutifully believing his work
is appreciated. Eventually even Bob starts to burn out.

But what can Bob do? It's hard for him to cut down, because he's not
officially doing any of the work he is actually doing. When he goes to
HR, they flatly insist that, no, he's not actually even involved in
the projects which he's been working on for the last five years.

By this time, Bob has been working in a position way above his pay
grade for many years. If he quit, he'd be back a decade of work,
leaving behind the castles that he's nurtured and built, applying for
positions well below the level he's actually been working at (even if
that's unacknowledged). In fact, he can't even talk about the work
he's been doing for a significant fraction of his life, or it will be
contradicted by the official record and Eve. And by this point Eve is
telling him point blank not to talk about all the work he's
done. After all it's far in excess of what anyone 'should' have done -
and he doesn't want to be in trouble for violating company policy or
the workplace regulations. So Bob continues.

In the end it isnt the level of the level of physical work that gets
to Bob, it is his inner life. Bob is older now, and working such long
working weeks (and weekends) is physically taking its toll. But what
grates to Bob is struggling to cope mentally. He is doing work today
that tomorrow Alice will claim she did, and he knows it. He knows he
won't be able to say tomorrow what he did today. He's living a shadow
of a real life - where he almost doesn't exist. Bob regrets all the
sacrifice that he's made- all that hard work over many years- for
others gain.

I don't know what became of Bob, but he certainly no longer believes
in working hard to get ahead. He spent his life doing that, and wishes
that someone had warned him when he was young, that it doesn't
necessarily follow.

Bob's case is just a story. But in my observation, in any sizeable
organisation, whether it's government or private, if it's big enough,
there will always a large number of 'Eves': People who are better at
climbing the greasy pole than actually providing anything of value;
People who are not constrained to act in moral ways, such as
accurately attributing work, if that gets in the way of their own
advancement; People who are better at taking credit for a task, than
doing the task in the first place.

I wish it wasn't that way, but that is the way our world is.
